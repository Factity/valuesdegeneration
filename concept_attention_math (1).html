Concepts on the Fringe: Every Formula in the Notebook, Variable by Variable

This page explains concept_attention_dynamics.ipynb. Every formula is followed by a where list that names each symbol and says what it is in the notebook. Settings are the notebook defaults: $600$ sentences per generation, $12$ generations, $4$ runs per condition.

1. What the notebook does

  1. Reads the Sherlock books: the first $80\%$ of each is readable, the last $20\%$ is held out.
  2. Turns words into vectors, then groups common words into $150$ concepts.
  3. Defines relations between concepts, the fringe (rare relations) and the hidden truths (relations only in held-out text).
  4. Builds a small attention language model and runs the self-consuming loop: each generation reads a share $u$ of text written by the previous one.
  5. Measures ten named variables per generation and fits a sparse dynamical system for how they change.

2. Words become vectors

$$\mathrm{PPMI}(i,j)=\max\!\left\{0,\ \log\frac{\#(i,j)/T}{\big(\#(i)/T\big)\,\hat P_{0.75}(j)}\right\}$$
  • $\#(i,j)$ co-occurrence count: how often word $j$ appears within $4$ words of word $i$ in the same sentence.
  • $\#(i)$ how often word $i$ is the centre word, $\sum_j\#(i,j)$.
  • $T$ total number of co-occurrences, $\sum_{i,j}\#(i,j)$.
  • $\hat P_{0.75}(j)$ smoothed context probability, $\#(j)^{0.75}/\sum_k\#(k)^{0.75}$; the exponent $0.75$ stops very frequent words dominating.
  • $\mathrm{PPMI}(i,j)$ positive pointwise mutual information: how much more often the pair occurs than chance, floored at $0$.
$$\mathrm{PPMI}\approx U\Sigma V^{\top},\qquad e_i=\frac{U_i\,\Sigma^{1/2}}{\big\|U_i\,\Sigma^{1/2}\big\|}$$
  • $U,\Sigma,V$ truncated singular value decomposition keeping the $32$ largest singular values.
  • $U_i$ row $i$ of $U$, the coordinates of word $i$.
  • $e_i$ the embedding of word $i$: a unit vector of $32$ numbers. Words with similar meaning have a high cosine $e_i^{\top}e_j$. The table is fixed during the loop.

3. Concepts

A content word is not a stopword, not a proper name (capitalised in mid-sentence at least $60\%$ of the time), has $\ge3$ letters, occurs $\ge5$ times, and appears in at most $2\%$ of sentences (DF_MAX; generic words such as "said" cannot name an idea). Content words are clustered by weighted $k$-means:

$$\min_{\{\mu_c\}}\ \sum_{w}\log(1+n_w)\,\big\|e_w-\mu_{c(w)}\big\|^{2},\qquad \bar\mu_c=\frac{\mu_c}{\|\mu_c\|}$$
  • $w$ a content word; $e_w$ its embedding.
  • $n_w$ how many times $w$ occurs in the readable text; $\log(1+n_w)$ stops very common words dominating a cluster.
  • $c(w)\in\{1,\dots,150\}$ the concept assigned to $w$ ($150$ concepts, N_CONCEPTS).
  • $\mu_c$ centre of concept $c$; $\bar\mu_c$ its unit-length direction.

Each concept is named by the four words nearest to $\bar\mu_c$ and by the most common WordNet category of its words.

4. Relations, fringe and hidden truth

$$e=\{a,b\}\ \text{is a relation in sentence } s\iff \exists\,p
  • $e=\{a,b\}$ a relation: an unordered pair of two different concepts.
  • $w_p$ the word at position $p$ of sentence $s$; $c(w_p)$ its concept.
  • $6$ the window (REL_WINDOW): the two words must be at most $6$ words apart. A relation counts once per sentence.
$$S_0=\{e:w^{\mathrm{tr}}_e>0\},\quad \mathcal F=\{e\in S_0:\ w^{\mathrm{tr}}_e\le2\},\quad \mathcal H=\{e:\ w^{\mathrm{hid}}_e>0,\ e\notin S_0,\ e\subseteq\mathrm{known}\},\quad \mathcal T=S_0\cup\mathcal H$$
  • $w^{\mathrm{tr}}_e$ number of readable sentences containing relation $e$; $w^{\mathrm{hid}}_e$ the same in the held-out text.
  • $S_0$ the real relations.
  • $\mathcal F$ the fringe: real relations seen at most twice (FRINGE_PAIR_COUNT).
  • $\mathrm{known}$ concepts that occur in some relation of $S_0$.
  • $\mathcal H$ the hidden truths: relations that appear only in held-out text, between known concepts.
  • $\mathcal T$ everything counted as true.
$$\pi_0(c)=\frac{n^{\mathrm{tr}}_c}{\sum_{c'}n^{\mathrm{tr}}_{c'}},\qquad m_e=\frac{\bar\mu_a+\bar\mu_b}{\|\bar\mu_a+\bar\mu_b\|}$$
  • $\pi_0$ the real concept distribution; $n^{\mathrm{tr}}_c$ content tokens of concept $c$ in the readable text.
  • $m_e$ the meaning vector of relation $e=\{a,b\}$: the normalised midpoint of its two concept directions.

5. The attention model

A sentence is $\langle s\rangle,w_1,\dots,w_m,\langle/s\rangle$ with embeddings $e_0,\dots,e_{m+1}$. Position $t$ has two attention heads.

$$a_{tj}=\mathrm{softmax}_{j\le t}\Big(g\,e_t^{\top}e_j-\lambda\,(t-j)\Big),\qquad c_t=\sum_{j\le t}a_{tj}\,e_j$$
  • $t$ the current position; $j\le t$ an earlier (or equal) position it may attend to.
  • $a_{tj}$ the context-head attention weight from position $t$ to $j$ (sums to $1$ over $j$).
  • $g=4$ gain: how strongly similar words attract attention. $\lambda=0.25$ distance penalty: how fast attention fades with distance.
  • $c_t$ the context vector: an attention-weighted average of the earlier words' embeddings.

The previous-token head attends only to position $t-1$ and returns $e_{t-1}$ (zero at the start); it keeps local word order. The heads and the current word form one query (stored text forms keys the same way):

$$q_t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+w^{2}+\omega^{2}}}\Big[\,e_t\ ;\ w\,e_{t-1}\ ;\ \omega\,\hat c_t\,\Big],\qquad \hat c_t=\frac{c_t}{\|c_t\|}$$
  • $q_t$ the query; the semicolons mean the three parts are joined into one vector.
  • $w=0.8$ weight of the previous-token part; $\omega=0.7$ weight of the context part. The factor in front makes $q_t$ unit length.

Training is storage. Every position $i$ of the training sentences becomes a slot $(k_i,v_i,n_i)$. Only the $K=4000$ slots with the largest $n_i$ are kept (ties at random), so rare material is dropped first: this is the model's finite capacity.

$$\alpha_i=\mathrm{softmax}_i\big(\beta\,q^{\top}k_i+\gamma\log n_i\big),\qquad o=\sum_i\alpha_i\,v_i,\qquad p(w)=\mathrm{softmax}_w\!\Big(\frac{e_w^{\top}o}{\tau}\Big)$$
  • $k_i$ the stored key (the query computed at training position $i$); $v_i$ the embedding of the word that followed.
  • $n_i$ how many training positions share position $i$'s (word, next word) pair; $\gamma\log n_i$ with $\gamma=1$ favours common patterns.
  • $\beta=120$ sharpness of the memory read; $\alpha_i$ the memory attention weight on slot $i$.
  • $o$ the blended next-word vector. Because it blends several slots, the model can write a word near in meaning to what it stored.
  • $\tau=0.04$ output temperature (TAU_OUT): higher means blurrier, more novel and less accurate. $p(w)$ the probability of writing word $w$ next.

6. The self-consuming loop

$$n_s=\mathrm{round}(u\,n),\quad n_r=n-n_s,\qquad \mathrm{Train}_t=\underbrace{n_r\ \text{real sentences}}_{\text{drawn at random}}\ \cup\ \underbrace{\mathrm{Pool}_{t-1}[1..n_s]}_{\text{written by generation }t-1}$$
  • $n=600$ sentences per generation; $u\in[0,1]$ the synthetic share (the control): $u=0$ no loop, $u=1$ fully synthetic.
  • $n_s,n_r$ numbers of synthetic and fresh real sentences.
  • $\mathrm{Pool}_t$ the $n$ sentences generation $t$ writes; it is both the source of the next generation's synthetic text and the text on which generation $t$ is measured. Generation $0$ reads $n$ real sentences.

7. The ten variables

$D_t$Data seensentence$|\mathrm{Seen}_t|/N_R$
$C_t$Capture rateword$\frac1P\sum_i\mathbf 1\big[y_i\in\mathrm{top5}\,p(\cdot\mid q_i)\big]$
$R_t$Concept retrievalconcept$1-\tfrac12\sum_c|\pi_t(c)-\pi_0(c)|$
$Q_t$Context use (reasoning vs knee-jerk)attention$\frac1P\sum_i\mathrm{TV}\big(p(\cdot\mid q_i),\,p(\cdot\mid q_i^{\sigma})\big)$
$F_t$Fringe retrievalconcept$|\mathrm{rel}_t\cap\mathcal F|/|\mathcal F|$
$A_t$Fringe accuracyword$|\mathrm{tail}_t\cap\mathrm{real}|/|\mathrm{tail}_t|$
$N_t$Concept noveltyconcept$\sum_{e\in\mathrm{novel}_t}c_t(e)\big/\sum_e c_t(e)$
$M_t$Novelty–fringe proximityconcept$\sum_{e\in\mathrm{novel}_t}c_t(e)\max_{f\in\mathcal F}m_e^{\top}m_f\big/\sum_{e\in\mathrm{novel}_t}c_t(e)$
$V_t$Validated discoveryconcept$|\mathrm{rel}_t\cap\mathcal H|/|\mathcal H|$
$S_t$Sentence diversitysentence$\min\{1,\ \bar d_t/\bar d_{\mathrm{real}}\}$
  • $\mathrm{Seen}_t$ real training sentences drawn into any training set so far; $N_R$ the number of real training sentences.
  • $P=300$ probe positions taken from the model's own training text; $q_i$ the query at probe $i$; $y_i$ the word that really followed; $\mathrm{top5}$ the five most probable next words.
  • $q_i^{\sigma}$ probe $i$ with its context part swapped for that of another random probe $\sigma(i)$; $\mathrm{TV}(p,p')=\tfrac12\sum_w|p(w)-p'(w)|$ total variation between two next-word distributions. If $Q_t=0$ the model ignores attended context.
  • $\pi_t$ concept distribution of $\mathrm{Pool}_t$ (same definition as $\pi_0$).
  • $\mathrm{rel}_t$ relations present in $\mathrm{Pool}_t$; $c_t(e)$ how often relation $e$ occurs there; $\mathrm{novel}_t=\mathrm{rel}_t\setminus S_0$ relations never seen in training.
  • $\mathrm{tail}_t$ word-pair types in $\mathrm{Pool}_t$ whose count is at or below the median count; $\mathrm{real}$ word pairs that occur in the real text, readable or held out.
  • $m_e,m_f$ meaning vectors of a novel relation and a fringe relation, so $M_t$ is high when new claims lie close to rare knowledge.
  • $\mathcal F,\mathcal H$ as in section 4.

Sentence diversity uses attention pooling of a sentence's content words:

$$b_i=\mathrm{softmax}_i\!\Big(\frac{e_i^{\top}\bar e}{0.25}\Big),\qquad v_s=\frac{\sum_i b_i\,e_i}{\big\|\sum_i b_i\,e_i\big\|},\qquad \bar d=\mathbb E\big[\,1-v_s^{\top}v_{s'}\,\big]$$
  • $i$ a content word of the sentence; $\bar e$ the normalised mean of their embeddings (the sentence's overall meaning).
  • $b_i$ the pooling weight: words closest to the sentence's meaning count most. $v_s$ the sentence vector.
  • $\bar d$ mean cosine distance between two random sentences' vectors ($3000$ pairs); $\bar d_t$ for $\mathrm{Pool}_t$ and $\bar d_{\mathrm{real}}$ for real text. Low $S_t$ means the model keeps writing the same kind of sentence.

8. The dynamical system

$$z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma},\qquad \tilde u=\frac{u-\bar u}{s_u},\qquad z_{t+1}=z_t+\Theta(z_t,\tilde u)\,\Xi,\qquad \Theta=\big[\,1,\ z,\ \tilde u,\ z\,\tilde u,\ \tilde u^{2}\,\big]$$
  • $x$ the vector of the ten variables of section 7 for one generation; $\mu,\sigma$ their mean and standard deviation over the training runs; $z$ the standardised state.
  • $\bar u,s_u$ mean and standard deviation of the training synthetic shares; $\tilde u$ the standardised control.
  • $\Theta$ the library of $23$ candidate terms: a constant, each state, the control, each state times the control, and the control squared. Products of two different states are left out because $240$ training rows cannot support $45$ more terms.
  • $\Xi$ the $23\times10$ coefficient matrix to be found; most entries end up exactly zero.
  • $z_{t+1}-z_t$ the change in one generation. A zero column of $\Xi$ means that variable does not change.
$$w_{\mathcal S}=\big(\tilde\Theta_{\mathcal S}^{\top}\tilde\Theta_{\mathcal S}+\alpha\,m\,I\big)^{-1}\tilde\Theta_{\mathcal S}^{\top}Y_{\cdot k},\qquad \text{drop term }j\text{ if }|w_j|<\theta\,\max_{j'}|w^{(0)}_{j'}|$$
  • $\mathcal S$ the active (kept) terms; $\tilde\Theta$ the library with columns scaled to unit rms; $m$ number of training rows.
  • $Y_{\cdot k}$ the one-generation changes of variable $k$; $\alpha\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ ridge strength; $I$ the identity matrix.
  • $w^{(0)}$ the first fit with all terms; $\theta\in\{0.05,\dots,0.60\}$ the relative threshold. Fitting and dropping repeat until the set stops changing. An equation explaining under $1\%$ of the change is set to zero.
$$\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{fr}}=\frac1{|\mathcal V|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}\frac1{(T+1)K}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\big\|\hat z_t(u)-\bar z_t(u)\big\|^{2},\qquad \mathrm{skill}_{k}=1-\frac{\sum_t(\hat z_{t,k}-\bar z_{t,k})^2}{\sum_t(\bar z_{0,k}-\bar z_{t,k})^2}$$
  • $\hat z_t(u)$ the fitted system free-run from the generation-0 state (no correction); states beyond $\pm6$ are clipped and counted.
  • $\bar z_t(u)$ the average real trajectory at share $u$; $\mathcal V$ the validation shares $\{0.4,0.9\}$; $T=12$ generations; $K=10$ variables.
  • $\mathrm{skill}_k$ for variable $k$ on test shares $\{0.1,0.6,0.95\}$: $1$ perfect, $0$ no better than "nothing changes", negative worse.
  • Models are ranked by fewest clipped steps (including a $36$-generation stability run), then lowest $\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{fr}}$, then the sparsest within $10\%$ of the best.

9. Sweeps

$$\mathrm{Retention}(u)=\frac{\bar F_T(u)}{\bar F_0(u)},\qquad \mathrm{Yield}=\frac{|\mathrm{rel}_T\cap\mathcal H|}{|\mathrm{novel}_T|}$$
  • $\bar F_t(u)$ the mean fringe retrieval at share $u$ and generation $t$; Retention is the share of the generation-0 fringe left after $T$ generations.
  • Yield proved relations per new relation written, computed while the output temperature $\tau$ is varied over $\{0.02,\dots,0.09\}$ at $u=0.5$.

10. What my test run showed, and limits

I could not download the Sherlock texts, so I ran the notebook on other novels (Austen, Chesterton, Melville and similar). It checks the machinery, not Sherlock.

  • Collapse at $u=1$: $S$ fell from about $0.9$ to $0.05$, $R$ from $0.6$ to $0.03$, $Q$ and $F$ to $0$, while $C$ rose to $0.99$. The model wrote near-identical phrases ("think not be such a whale") and a few concepts took over, one from $1.2\%$ to about $33\%$ of the text.
  • $u=0$: stable apart from $D$, which accumulates by design. Fringe: only $u=0$ kept half of the generation-0 fringe relations.
  • Fitted system: $79$ of $230$ terms, no clipping, free-run skill $+0.82$, $+0.85$, $+0.79$ at $u=0.1,0.6,0.95$; one-step skill only $+0.23$. Good for $R,F,V,C,Q$, weak for $N$ and $A$ (about $-0.9$ to $+0.1$) and for $S$ at $u=0.95$.
  • Limits: training is storage and embeddings are fixed, so this is a toy attention model; concepts are word clusters; "proved" means present in held-out text; fringe retention is a proxy, not a measure of values; twelve generations and four runs compare conditions but do not give constants.
